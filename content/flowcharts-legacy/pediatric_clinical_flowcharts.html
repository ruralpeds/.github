Pediatric Trauma — Primary Survey
ATLS-based systematic approach with pediatric modifications
PEDIATRIC TRAUMA ACTIVATION — Significant Mechanism of Injury
▼
A — AIRWAY with C-Spine Protection
Jaw thrust (no head tilt) · Shoulder roll for infant (large occiput) · Suction · OPA/NPA
RSI with in-line stabilization if needed (Ketamine 1–2 mg/kg + Rocuronium 1 mg/kg)
▼
B — BREATHING
Expose chest · Auscultate bilateral · Assess tracheal position · SpO2
Needle decompression if tension pneumothorax (2nd ICS MCL or 4th/5th ICS AAL)
Chest tube for hemothorax/pneumothorax (10–32 Fr by age)
▼
C — CIRCULATION with Hemorrhage Control
Direct pressure on external bleeding · IV/IO access ×2 · Labs (T&S, CBC, lactate, BG, coags)
NS/LR 10–20 mL/kg bolus → Reassess → Repeat to 40 mL/kg max
If no improvement → Activate MTP → pRBC 10 mL/kg · TXA 15–20 mg/kg (max 1 g)
▼
D — DISABILITY
GCS (age-modified) · Pupil exam · Glucose check · Lateralizing signs
▼
E — EXPOSURE & ENVIRONMENT
Fully expose → Logroll → Prevent hypothermia (warmed fluids, Bair Hugger, warm room)
FAST exam if available · Pelvic stability · Long bone assessment
▼
HEMODYNAMICALLY STABLE?
YES
Secondary Survey
Head-to-toe exam · Imaging as available (XR, FAST) · Serial reassessment q15–30 min
Arrange transfer to trauma center
NO
Ongoing Resuscitation
Blood products · Vasoactives if needed · Damage-control resuscitation principles
Identify source: chest, abdomen, pelvis, external, long bone
Emergent surgical consult · EXPEDITE transfer
REASSESS q15 min: HR, BP, Cap Refill, UOP, Lactate, Mental Status

Hemorrhagic Shock
Damage-control resuscitation in the rural setting
SIGNS OF HEMORRHAGIC SHOCK — Tachycardia, Poor Perfusion, Altered MS
▼
Immediate Actions
Direct pressure on external hemorrhage · 2 large-bore IV or IO access
Type & Screen/Crossmatch STAT · POC Hgb/lactate · Activate blood bank
▼
1st Bolus: NS or LR 10–20 mL/kg over 5–10 min
TXA: 15–20 mg/kg IV (max 1 g) over 10 min — within 3 hrs of injury
▼
CLINICAL IMPROVEMENT?
↑BP, ↓HR, improved cap refill, improved mental status
YES — RESPONDER
Continue crystalloid to restore perfusion
Monitor q15 min · Serial Hgb/lactate
Prepare for transfer
NO — AFTER 40 mL/kg
Fluid-Refractory Hemorrhagic Shock
▼
Activate Massive Transfusion Protocol
pRBC 10 mL/kg (O-neg or type-specific) · Target Hgb 7–10 g/dL
If available: FFP 10 mL/kg + Platelets · Goal ratio pRBC:FFP:Plt = 1:1:1
TXA infusion: 2 mg/kg/hr (max 125 mg/hr) for 8 hrs
▼
Monitor for Lethal Triad: Hypothermia + Acidosis + Coagulopathy
Warm all fluids/blood · Target temp >36°C · Check ABG/coags q30–60 min
Correct Ca²⁺ (0.6 mL/kg Ca gluconate 10%) after every 40 mL/kg blood products
REASSESS q5–15 min: HR, BP, Cap Refill, Lactate, Hgb, Coags, Temp
3
Traumatic Brain Injury
Prevention of secondary brain injury — sustained management
HEAD TRAUMA with Altered Mental Status or Neurologic Deficit
▼
Rapid Assessment
GCS (pediatric-modified for <2 yr) · Pupils (size, reactivity, symmetry)
Lateralizing signs · Signs of basilar skull fracture (raccoon eyes, Battle sign, hemotympanum)
C-spine precautions · Glucose check
▼
GCS ≤ 8 or Signs of Herniation?
Herniation = Cushing triad (↑BP, ↓HR, irregular resp), fixed dilated pupil, posturing
GCS ≤ 8
Definitive Airway — RSI
Ketamine 1–2 mg/kg + Rocuronium 1 mg/kg
Avoid hypotension during induction
Target: SpO₂ >94%, PaCO₂ 35–40
HERNIATION
EMERGENT ICP Reduction
3% HTS 2–5 mL/kg IV over 10–20 min (preferred)
OR Mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg over 15–20 min
Brief hyperventilation to PaCO₂ 30–35 (temporizing ONLY)
▼
Sustained TBI Management Targets
SpO₂ >94% (PaO₂ >60) · PaCO₂ 35–40 mmHg (avoid hypo- and hypercapnia)
SBP > age-appropriate 5th percentile · Glucose 80–180 mg/dL · Temp 36–37.5°C
HOB 30° · Head midline · Avoid jugular vein compression · Seizure prophylaxis if GCS ≤8
▼
Seizure Prophylaxis: Levetiracetam 40–60 mg/kg IV (max 3 g)
Hyperosmolar Therapy: 3% HTS 2–5 mL/kg PRN for ↑ICP signs (may repeat)
Mannitol: hold if serum osm >320 · Ensure adequate volume status before mannitol
REASSESS q15–30 min: GCS, Pupils, Vitals, Blood Gas, Glucose · Expedite Transfer to Neurosurgical Center
Avoid: Hypotension (most harmful secondary insult), Hypoxia, Hyperthermia, Hyperglycemia, Hyponatremia, Seizures

Pediatric Burns
Resuscitation, airway, and fluid management
BURN INJURY — Stop Burning Process, Remove Clothing
▼
Assess TBSA (Lund-Browder chart for children)
Assess Depth: Superficial · Partial thickness · Full thickness
Identify Special Burns: Face, hands, feet, genitalia, circumferential, electrical, chemical, inhalation
▼
Signs of Inhalation Injury?
Facial burns, singed nasal hair, soot in oropharynx, hoarseness, stridor, carbonaceous sputum
YES
EARLY INTUBATION
Do not delay — progressive edema makes later intubation impossible
100% FiO₂ · SpO₂ unreliable (CO poisoning)
CO-oximetry if available · COHb level
NO
Supplemental O₂ as needed
Monitor for delayed airway edema
Low threshold to intubate if any stridor develops
▼
Fluid Resuscitation (Burns >10% TBSA)
Parkland Formula (Pediatric): 3–4 mL × kg × %TBSA = total over 24 hrs
Give ½ in first 8 hrs from time of injury · ½ over next 16 hrs
Children <20 kg: ADD maintenance fluids with dextrose (D5NS at maintenance rate)
Target UOP: 1 mL/kg/hr (<30 kg) or 0.5–1 mL/kg/hr (>30 kg)
▼
Wound Care: Cool water (not ice) to burns <10% TBSA · Cover with clean dry dressing
Pain: Morphine 0.1 mg/kg IV or Ketamine 0.3 mg/kg IV · Avoid NSAIDs initially
Tetanus: Update if not current · Escharotomy for circumferential full-thickness burns with vascular compromise
REASSESS q1h: UOP, Vitals, Fluid Rate Adjustment · Transfer to Burn Center

Compartment Syndrome
Recognition and emergent management
EXTREMITY INJURY — Fracture, Crush, or Reperfusion Mechanism
▼
Clinical Assessment
Pain out of proportion to injury (MOST RELIABLE early sign)
Pain with passive stretch of involved compartment
Increasing analgesic requirements · Tense/firm compartment on palpation
Late signs (less reliable): Paresthesias, Pallor, Pulselessness
▼
Challenges in Children: Preverbal patients cannot report pain · Obtunded patients unreliable
Increasing agitation/distress despite adequate analgesia is a RED FLAG
The 3 A's: Anxiety, Agitation, increasing Analgesic need → suspect compartment syndrome
▼
Immediate Actions:
Remove ALL circumferential dressings, splints, casts · Elevate limb to heart level (not above)
Reassess perfusion after release · Compartment pressure measurement if device available
Pressure >30 mmHg or within 30 mmHg of diastolic → confirms diagnosis
▼
Transfer to surgical facility feasible within 6 hours?
YES
Aggressive pain management
Serial neurovascular checks q30 min
EMERGENT transfer — time to fasciotomy is critical
NO — PROLONGED DELAY
Consider Bedside Fasciotomy
Phone/video guidance from orthopedic surgeon
Full-length skin incision over involved compartment
Definitive treatment cannot be delayed >6 hrs
Neurovascular Checks q30 min: Pulse, Sensation, Motor, Pain Level, Compartment Tension

Respiratory Failure — Stepwise Escalation
Progressive respiratory support ladder
RESPIRATORY DISTRESS — ↑WOB, Tachypnea, ↓SpO₂, Abnormal PAT
▼
Rapid Assessment
Pediatric Assessment Triangle (Appearance, WOB, Circulation)
Type I (hypoxemic: PaO₂ <60, SpO₂ <90) vs Type II (hypercapnic: PaCO₂ >50)
Auscultation · SpO₂ · Capnography if available
▼
STEP 1 — Supplemental O₂
NC 0.25–4 L/min · Simple mask 6–10 L/min · NRB 10–15 L/min
Target SpO₂ 92–97% (neonates 94–99%)
▼
Not improving
STEP 2 — High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC)
Infants: 2 L/kg/min (max 8 L/min) · Children: 1–2 L/kg/min (max 50–70 L/min)
Heated, humidified · Creates low-level positive pressure
▼
Not improving
STEP 3 — NIPPV (CPAP/BiPAP)
CPAP: Start 5–8 cm H₂O · BiPAP: IPAP 10–12, EPAP 5–6 cm H₂O
Contraindicated: Unable to protect airway, hemodynamic instability, undrained PTX
▼
Not improving or unable to tolerate
STEP 4 — Endotracheal Intubation
RSI protocol (see Flowchart 7) · Indications: failure of non-invasive support,
inability to protect airway, impending arrest, need for reliable airway for transport
▼
Post-Intubation Ventilator Settings
Mode: PC-SIMV or VC-SIMV · Vt 6–8 mL/kg IBW · PEEP 5 cm H₂O
Rate: age-appropriate · FiO₂: wean to <0.60 · Plateau <30 cm H₂O
Blood gas in 15–30 min → adjust
REASSESS at each step: SpO₂, WOB, RR, Mental Status, ABG if available

Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)
The 7 P's framework — rural pediatric adaptation
DECISION TO INTUBATE
▼
P1 — PREPARATION (SOAP-ME)
Suction (Yankauer + flexible) · Oxygen (BVM + mask, 15 L/min flow) · Airway (laryngoscope + blade tested, ETT + stylet, 1 size above/below, LMA backup) · Pharmacology (induction + paralytic drawn, doses verified ×2) · Monitoring/Equipment (SpO₂, ETCO₂, cardiac monitor)
▼
P2 — PREOXYGENATION
100% FiO₂ for 3–5 min via NRB or BVM without positive pressure if possible
Apneic oxygenation: NC at max flow during intubation attempt
▼
P3 — PRETREATMENT (optional)
Glycopyrrolate 0.004–0.01 mg/kg (max 0.2 mg) for secretions (especially infants)
Lidocaine 1.5 mg/kg IV for ↑ICP · Atropine 0.02 mg/kg for age <1 yr (controversial)
▼
P4 — PARALYSIS with INDUCTION
Induction: Ketamine 1–2 mg/kg IV (preferred — hemodynamic stability, bronchodilation)
Paralytic: Rocuronium 1 mg/kg IV (onset 45–60 sec; preferred over succinylcholine)
Wait for jaw relaxation (45–60 sec)
▼
P5 — PLACEMENT with PROOF
Laryngoscopy → Visualize cords → Pass ETT to calculated depth
Confirm: ETCO₂ waveform (GOLD STANDARD) · Bilateral breath sounds · Chest rise · SpO₂
Secure with tape + commercial holder · Note depth at lip
▼
P6 — POST-INTUBATION MANAGEMENT
Initiate ventilator · Sedation infusion · Chest X-ray for tube position
Blood gas in 15–30 min · NG/OG tube placement
▼
P7 — PLAN B (if intubation fails)
BVM ventilation → Reposition → Reattempt (max 3 attempts) → LMA insertion
If cannot intubate AND cannot oxygenate: Surgical airway
<8–10 yr: Needle cricothyrotomy + jet ventilation · ≥10 yr: Surgical cricothyrotomy

Status Asthmaticus
Escalating bronchodilator and adjunctive therapy
SEVERE ASTHMA EXACERBATION — Refractory to Initial Bronchodilator
▼
TIER 1 — Standard Therapy (Immediate)
Continuous albuterol nebulization 0.5 mg/kg/hr (max 15 mg/hr)
Ipratropium 250–500 mcg neb × first 3 treatments
Dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg IV/IM (max 16 mg) OR Methylprednisolone 2 mg/kg IV (max 60 mg)
Supplemental O₂ to maintain SpO₂ >92%
▼
Persistent distress after 30–60 min
TIER 2 — Escalation
Magnesium sulfate 25–75 mg/kg IV over 20 min (max 2 g) — single dose
Continue continuous albuterol · Reassess frequently
▼
Still refractory
TIER 3 — Refractory Status Asthmaticus
IV Terbutaline: Load 10 mcg/kg over 10 min → Infusion 0.1–10 mcg/kg/min
(Monitor: cardiac rhythm, lactate, K⁺ — terbutaline causes tachycardia, hypokalemia)
IV Ketamine: 0.5–2 mg/kg bolus → 0.5–2 mg/kg/hr infusion (bronchodilatory properties)
Heliox 70:30 or 80:20 if available
▼
Respiratory failure
INTUBATION — LAST RESORT in Asthma
High risk of cardiovascular collapse from ↑ intrathoracic pressure
Induction: Ketamine 1–2 mg/kg (NOT propofol) + Rocuronium 1 mg/kg
Ventilator: Low rate (8–12) · I:E 1:3 to 1:4 · Vt 6–8 mL/kg
Permissive hypercapnia (accept PaCO₂ 60–80 if pH >7.15) · Low PEEP (3–5)
REASSESS q15–30 min: SpO₂, WOB, Air Entry, Peak Pressures, ABG, K⁺, Lactate

Croup (Laryngotracheobronchitis)
Severity assessment and stepwise management
BARKY COUGH, STRIDOR, HOARSENESS — Typical Age 6 mo – 3 yr
▼
SEVERITY ASSESSMENT (Westley Croup Score)
MILD
Occasional barky cough
No stridor at rest
No/mild retractions

Tx: Dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg PO (max 16 mg)
Observe 2–4 hrs → Discharge if improved
MODERATE
Frequent barky cough
Stridor at rest
Visible retractions

Tx: Dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg PO/IV/IM
Racemic epi 0.05 mL/kg of 2.25% (max 0.5 mL) neb
Observe ≥2–4 hrs post racemic epi
SEVERE
Stridor at rest, severe retractions
Altered mental status or cyanosis

Tx: Dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg IV/IM
Racemic epi neb — may repeat q15–20 min
Blow-by O₂ · Minimize agitation
Prepare for intubation: ETT 0.5–1 mm smaller than predicted
Admit or transfer
Red Flags for Alternative Diagnosis: No viral prodrome, toxic-appearing, drooling, tripod position → Consider epiglottitis, bacterial tracheitis, retropharyngeal abscess. Do not examine throat if epiglottitis suspected.

Bronchiolitis
Supportive care and escalation pathway
INFANT WITH WHEEZING/CRACKLES, RHINORRHEA, COUGH — Typical Age <2 yr
▼
Identify High-Risk Features
Age <2 months · Former premature infant · Congenital heart disease
Chronic lung disease · Immunodeficiency · Apnea history or observed apnea
▼
Supportive Care (Mainstay of Treatment)
Nasal suctioning (bulb or mechanical) · Supplemental O₂ to maintain SpO₂ >90–92%
Ensure adequate hydration (PO or IV) · Elevate HOB slightly · Minimize handling
Bronchodilators and steroids have limited evidence in typical bronchiolitis
▼
Worsening Respiratory Distress Despite Supportive Care?
MODERATE — Escalate
HFNC: 2 L/kg/min (max 8 L/min in infants)
Continuous SpO₂ and cardiorespiratory monitoring
Consider trial of albuterol in wheezing infants >6 mo — discontinue if no response
SEVERE — Respiratory Failure
CPAP 5–8 cm H₂O via nasal interface
If failing CPAP or apneic episodes → INTUBATE
Low threshold for intubation in young infants with apnea
Transfer to PICU
REASSESS q1–2h: SpO₂, RR, WOB, Feeding Tolerance, Hydration Status, Apnea Episodes
RSV Apnea: Young infants (<2 mo) with RSV may present with apnea as the primary symptom BEFORE significant lower respiratory symptoms develop. Mandate continuous monitoring.

Foreign Body Aspiration
Assessment and management by acuity
SUDDEN ONSET RESPIRATORY DISTRESS ± CHOKING HISTORY
▼
Able to Cough / Adequate Air Exchange?
YES — MILD OBSTRUCTION
Allow the child to cough — do NOT interfere
Supplemental O₂ · Continuous monitoring
Arrange URGENT transfer for bronchoscopy
Keep child calm · Avoid agitation
NO — SEVERE OBSTRUCTION
BLS Foreign Body Removal
▼
Age of Child?
INFANT (<1 YR)
5 back blows (heel of hand between scapulae, head lower than body)
+ 5 chest thrusts (2 fingers on sternum)
Alternate until object expelled or infant becomes unresponsive
CHILD (≥1 YR)
Abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver)
Repeat until object expelled or child becomes unresponsive
▼
If Unresponsive: Begin CPR · Open airway — look for visible object
If object visible → Remove with Magill forceps under direct laryngoscopy
If not visible → DO NOT blind finger sweep · Continue CPR · Attempt intubation (may push FB distally into one bronchus, enabling ventilation of the other lung)
▼
Definitive Treatment: Rigid bronchoscopy (not available in most rural settings)
Expedite transfer to facility with pediatric ENT/pulmonology capability

Pediatric Sepsis & Septic Shock
First-hour bundle and vasoactive escalation
SUSPECTED INFECTION + ORGAN DYSFUNCTION = SEPSIS (Time Zero)
▼
FIRST-HOUR BUNDLE — All Within 60 Minutes
① Blood culture (do not delay ABx) · ② Broad-spectrum IV ABx within 30 min (see age-based table)
③ POC glucose + lactate · ④ NS/LR 10–20 mL/kg bolus × repeat to 40–60 mL/kg if shock persists
⑤ Reassess after EACH bolus for improvement AND fluid overload signs
▼
Shock Persists After 40–60 mL/kg Fluid?
= FLUID-REFRACTORY SHOCK → Start Vasoactive Agent
▼
Identify Shock Phenotype
COLD SHOCK
Cool extremities, weak pulses, narrow pulse pressure, prolonged cap refill, mottled

→ EPINEPHRINE
Start 0.05 mcg/kg/min
Titrate up to 0.3 mcg/kg/min
WARM SHOCK
Warm extremities, bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure, flash cap refill

→ NOREPINEPHRINE
Start 0.05 mcg/kg/min
Titrate up to 2 mcg/kg/min
▼
Catecholamine-resistant shock
CATECHOLAMINE-RESISTANT SHOCK
Add Vasopressin 0.0003–0.002 units/kg/min · Consider stress-dose Hydrocortisone 1–2 mg/kg IV (max 100 mg)
Especially if: h/o chronic steroid use, purpura fulminans, refractory hypotension
Target: MAP ≥ age-appropriate, UOP >1 mL/kg/hr, lactate clearance >10%/2hr
REASSESS q5 min (active resus) → q15 min (stabilizing) → q30–60 min (stable): HR, BP, Cap Refill, UOP, Lactate, Mental Status

Neonatal Sepsis
Early-onset and late-onset neonatal sepsis management
NEONATE WITH SIGNS CONCERNING FOR SEPSIS
▼
Recognition — Often Subtle and Nonspecific
Temperature instability (hypothermia more common than fever in neonates)
Poor feeding · Lethargy · Apnea · Tachycardia · Hypoglycemia · Jaundice
Irritability · Mottled skin · Delayed capillary refill
▼
Early-Onset (<72 hrs of life) or Late-Onset (>72 hrs)?
EARLY-ONSET
Ampicillin 50 mg/kg q6–8h
+ Gentamicin 4–5 mg/kg q24h
(Add Cefotaxime if meningitis suspected)
Pathogens: GBS, E. coli, Listeria
Add Acyclovir 20 mg/kg q8h if HSV risk
LATE-ONSET
Vancomycin 15 mg/kg q8–12h
+ Gentamicin or Cefepime
Consider antifungal if: preterm, central line, prior ABx
Pathogens: CoNS, S. aureus, GNR, Candida
▼
Resuscitation Modifications for Neonates
Fluid boluses: 10 mL/kg (more conservative) · Reassess after each for hepatomegaly, ↑WOB
1st-line vasopressor: Epinephrine (evidence shifting away from dopamine)
Hydrocortisone 1 mg/kg IV for catecholamine-resistant neonatal shock
Meticulous glucose monitoring: D10W for hypoglycemia · Maintain 50–110 mg/dL
REASSESS q15–30 min: Perfusion, Temp, Glucose, UOP, Apnea Episodes · Transfer to NICU

Febrile Neonate (0–60 Days)
Risk stratification and empiric management
INFANT 0–60 DAYS WITH FEVER ≥ 38.0°C (Rectal)
▼
Age 0–28 Days?
YES — 0 to 28 DAYS
FULL SEPSIS EVALUATION — ALL PATIENTS
Blood culture · UA with culture · CSF (cell count, protein, glucose, culture, +HSV PCR)
CBC, CMP, CRP/Procalcitonin if available · Surface HSV cultures if risk factors

Empiric ABx: Ampicillin + Gentamicin (add Cefotaxime if meningitis suspected)
Add Acyclovir 20 mg/kg q8h if: vesicles, seizures, ↑LFTs, ↓plts, CSF pleocytosis, maternal HSV

ADMIT — No exceptions in this age group
29–60 DAYS
Risk Stratification
Apply PECARN Febrile Infant Rule or Step-by-Step approach
Blood culture · UA with culture · CBC, CRP/PCT
LP strongly recommended; required if ill-appearing
▼
29–60 Day Infant: Well-Appearing with Low-Risk Labs?
LOW RISK
Consider Ceftriaxone IM + close follow-up in 24 hrs
In rural setting with limited follow-up:
Err toward admission with empiric ABx and observation
Conservative approach is safest
HIGH RISK or ILL-APPEARING
Full sepsis workup including LP
Empiric ABx: Ampicillin + Ceftriaxone (or Cefotaxime)
Consider Acyclovir · Admit for observation
Transfer if deteriorating
Rural Consideration: In settings with limited follow-up capability, a conservative approach (admission + empiric antibiotics) is the safest strategy for all febrile neonates 0–60 days regardless of risk stratification results.

Status Epilepticus
Time-based escalation protocol
SEIZURE ACTIVITY ≥ 5 MINUTES or Repeated Seizures Without Recovery
▼
TIME 0–5 min: STABILIZATION
Airway positioning · O₂ · Protect from injury · IV/IO access
CHECK GLUCOSE → If <60 mg/dL: D10W 2–4 mL/kg IV
Continuous SpO₂ and cardiac monitoring
▼
TIME 5–20 min: 1st LINE — BENZODIAZEPINES
IV/IO access: Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg) OR Midazolam 0.1–0.2 mg/kg (max 5 mg)
No IV access: Midazolam 0.2 mg/kg INTRANASAL (max 10 mg) or IM (max 10 mg)
OR Diazepam 0.5 mg/kg PR (max 20 mg)
May repeat ×1 at 5 min if seizure continues
▼
Seizure continues after 2 doses of benzo
TIME 20–40 min: 2nd LINE AGENTS
Option A: Levetiracetam 40–60 mg/kg IV over 15 min (max 3 g) — fewer hemodynamic effects
Option B: Fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg IV (max rate 150 mg PE/min) — cardiac monitor required
Option C (neonates): Phenobarbital 20 mg/kg IV (max rate 1 mg/kg/min) — respiratory depression risk
▼
Seizure continues = REFRACTORY STATUS
TIME 40+ min: REFRACTORY STATUS EPILEPTICUS
Additional 2nd-line dosing (Fosphenytoin 5–10 mg PE/kg) OR
Midazolam infusion 0.05–2 mg/kg/hr OR Ketamine 1–2 mg/kg bolus → 0.5–3 mg/kg/hr
INTUBATION almost always required at this stage
EXPEDITE TRANSFER — Pentobarbital coma beyond rural scope
REASSESS: Seizure activity, Airway, SpO₂, HR/BP, Glucose q15 min, Neuro exam between attempts
16
Altered Mental Status
AEIOU-TIPS systematic differential and approach
CHILD WITH ALTERED MENTAL STATUS — GCS <15 or Abnormal AVPU
▼
IMMEDIATE LIFE THREATS — Address First
Airway: Protect if GCS ≤8 → Intubate · Glucose: Check STAT → D10W 2–4 mL/kg if <60
Herniation signs (Cushing triad, blown pupil)? → 3% HTS 2–5 mL/kg + HOB 30°
Opioid exposure? → Naloxone 0.1 mg/kg IV/IN (max 2 mg)
▼
Systematic Differential — AEIOU-TIPS
Alcohol / Abuse (NAT) · Endocrine (DKA, adrenal crisis) · Insulin (hypoglycemia)
Opiates / Overdose · Uremia · Trauma · Infection (meningitis, encephalitis, sepsis)
Psychiatric · Seizure (postictal, non-convulsive status)
▼
Targeted Workup
POC glucose, blood gas, BMP, CBC, ammonia, lactate, LFTs, UDS, ethanol level
Blood/urine cultures if infection suspected · CT head if trauma or focal signs
LP if meningitis/encephalitis suspected (after CT if signs of ↑ICP)
EEG if non-convulsive status suspected (may not be available — treat empirically)
▼
Treat Identified Cause
Refer to disease-specific flowchart: DKA (#22) · Sepsis (#12) · Status Epilepticus (#15)
Meningitis (#17) · TBI (#3) · Toxicology (#24) · Adrenal Crisis (#23)
REASSESS q15–30 min: GCS, Pupils, Vitals, Glucose · Serial Neuro Exams
17
Meningitis & Encephalitis
Empiric treatment should never be delayed for LP
FEVER + ALTERED MENTAL STATUS ± NECK STIFFNESS ± SEIZURE
▼
Can LP Be Performed Immediately and Safely?
Contraindications to immediate LP: GCS ≤12, focal neuro signs, hemodynamic instability, coagulopathy, signs ↑ICP
YES — LP SAFE
Draw blood culture → LP → Send CSF (cell count, protein, glucose, Gram stain, culture, ± HSV PCR)
Then immediately start ABx
NO — LP DELAYED
Draw blood culture → ABx IMMEDIATELY
Do NOT delay antibiotics for LP or imaging
LP can be performed after stabilization if safe
▼
Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg IV (max 10 mg) — give BEFORE or WITH first ABx dose
(Reduces inflammation, hearing loss risk — benefit greatest when given early)

Empiric ABx: Ceftriaxone 50–100 mg/kg (meningitic dosing, max 4 g)
+ Vancomycin 15 mg/kg q6h (for resistant pneumococcus)
Neonates: Ampicillin + Cefotaxime (or Gentamicin)

Add Acyclovir 10–20 mg/kg IV q8h if: neonatal, temporal lobe focus, encephalitic features, CSF lymphocytic pleocytosis
REASSESS q1–2h: Neuro status, GCS, Pupils, Seizure activity, Vitals · Transfer to PICU

Pediatric Cardiac Arrest
AHA PALS algorithm — shockable vs non-shockable
UNRESPONSIVE · NO BREATHING · NO PULSE (or unsure within 10 sec)
▼
BEGIN HIGH-QUALITY CPR
Rate 100–120/min · Depth ≥⅓ AP diameter (4 cm infant, 5 cm child) · Full recoil
15:2 (2 rescuers) or 30:2 (1 rescuer) · Minimize interruptions (>80% CPR fraction)
Apply defibrillator pads · Establish IV/IO
▼
Rhythm Check — Shockable?
VF / pVT — SHOCKABLE
Shock #1: 2 J/kg → CPR 2 min
Shock #2: 4 J/kg → CPR 2 min → Epi 0.01 mg/kg IV/IO q3–5 min
Shock #3: 4 J/kg → CPR 2 min → Amiodarone 5 mg/kg (max 300 mg)
Continue cycle: Shock → CPR 2 min → Rhythm check
Epi q3–5 min · Amiodarone may repeat ×2 (max 15 mg/kg)
ASYSTOLE / PEA — NON-SHOCKABLE
Epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg IV/IO ASAP
→ CPR 2 min → Rhythm check
→ Repeat Epi q3–5 min
Search for reversible causes (H's & T's)
If rhythm becomes shockable → switch to left pathway
▼
H's and T's — Reversible Causes
H: Hypovolemia · Hypoxia · H⁺ (acidosis) · Hypo/Hyperkalemia · Hypothermia · Hypoglycemia
T: Tension pneumothorax · Tamponade · Toxins · Thrombosis (PE/coronary)
CPR Quality Targets
Rate: 100–120/minDepth: ≥⅓ AP diameter Full chest recoilFraction: >80% Epi max: 1 mg/doseAmio max: 300 mg 1st dose

Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)
Narrow complex tachycardia management
NARROW COMPLEX TACHYCARDIA — HR >220 (infant) or >180 (child)
▼
Hemodynamically Stable?
Signs of instability: hypotension, poor perfusion, altered mental status, signs of CHF
STABLE
Step 1 — Vagal Maneuvers
Infant: Ice to face (bag of ice water over forehead/bridge of nose for 15–30 sec)
Older child: Bearing down (Valsalva), blowing through obstructed straw
▼
No conversion
Step 2 — Adenosine
1st dose: 0.1 mg/kg rapid IV push (max 6 mg)
2nd dose: 0.2 mg/kg rapid IV push (max 12 mg)
Use IV closest to heart · 2-syringe rapid flush technique
▼
No conversion
Consider: Repeat adenosine · Synchronized cardioversion
Cardiology consultation
UNSTABLE
SYNCHRONIZED CARDIOVERSION
1st attempt: 0.5–1 J/kg
2nd attempt: 2 J/kg

Ketamine 1–2 mg/kg IV for sedation
if hemodynamics permit
DO NOT delay cardioversion for sedation
in critically unstable child
If SVT Recurs: Consider maintenance therapy — consult pediatric cardiology for agent selection (propranolol, flecainide, amiodarone depending on mechanism and age)
20
Myocarditis & Cardiogenic Shock
Recognition and inotropic support
SUSPECTED CARDIOGENIC SHOCK — ↓Perfusion, Tachycardia, Hepatomegaly, Gallop
▼
KEY DISTINGUISHING FEATURES (vs Septic Shock)
Hepatomegaly · Gallop rhythm · Jugular venous distension
Disproportionate tachycardia · WORSENS with fluid boluses
May mimic: respiratory illness, sepsis, abdominal disease
▼
CRITICAL: CAUTIOUS or NO FLUID ADMINISTRATION
Fluid may worsen pulmonary edema and cardiac output
If fluid given for initial undifferentiated shock: STOP if hepatomegaly or rales develop
Max trial: 5–10 mL/kg with very close reassessment
▼
Inotropic Support
Epinephrine 0.01–0.1 mcg/kg/min (most available in rural settings; titratable)
Milrinone 0.25–0.75 mcg/kg/min (ideal: inotrope + afterload reduction; causes vasodilation)
Diuresis: Furosemide 1 mg/kg IV if pulmonary edema present
Avoid: Negative inotropes (beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers)
▼
Diagnostics: 12-lead ECG (ST changes, low voltage, arrhythmias) · Troponin · BNP/NT-proBNP
POCUS/Echo if available (↓EF, ↓contractility, chamber dilation)
CXR: cardiomegaly, pulmonary edema · CBC, BMP, LFTs, coags
REASSESS q15 min: Perfusion, Hepatomegaly, Lung Sounds, UOP, Rhythm · URGENT Transfer to Pediatric Cardiac Center
21
Ductal-Dependent Congenital Heart Disease
Neonatal presentation and PGE1 management
NEONATE (Days to Weeks of Life) WITH CYANOSIS ± SHOCK
▼
Suspect Ductal-Dependent Lesion When:
Cyanosis unresponsive to supplemental O₂ · Shock without obvious infection source
4-extremity SpO₂ differential (pre-ductal ≠ post-ductal) · New or loud murmur
Absent or diminished femoral pulses · Metabolic acidosis · Collapse at 1–4 weeks of life
▼
START PROSTAGLANDIN E1 (Alprostadil)
Dose: 0.05–0.1 mcg/kg/min IV continuous infusion
Can be started PERIPHERALLY — do not delay for central access
May reduce to 0.01–0.025 mcg/kg/min once ductus reopens and patient improves
▼
PGE1 Side Effects — BE PREPARED
APNEA — occurs in up to 12% of neonates; have BVM and intubation equipment at bedside
Hypotension · Fever · Jitteriness · Flushing
Low threshold to electively intubate before transport if apnea occurs
▼
Supportive Care:
Avoid excessive O₂ in suspected mixing lesions (may worsen by reducing pulmonary vascular resistance)
Volume resuscitation if hypotensive (cautious 10 mL/kg boluses)
Correct acidosis, hypoglycemia, hypothermia · NPO · OG tube for gastric decompression
CONTINUOUS MONITORING: SpO₂, Cardiorespiratory, Apnea Watch · URGENT Transfer to Pediatric Cardiac Surgery Center
22
Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)
Fluid management, insulin, and cerebral edema watch
BG >200 + pH <7.3 (or HCO₃ <15) + Ketones = DKA
▼
Classify Severity
Mild: pH 7.2–7.3, HCO₃ 10–15 · Moderate: pH 7.1–7.2, HCO₃ 5–10 · Severe: pH <7.1, HCO₃ <5
▼
Initial Fluid Bolus
NS 10–20 mL/kg over 1 hour (limit to 10 mL/kg in severe DKA unless hemodynamically unstable)
Do NOT exceed 40 mL/kg in first 4 hours · Reassess perfusion after each bolus
▼
Insulin Infusion
Regular insulin 0.05–0.1 units/kg/hr IV infusion
NO IV INSULIN BOLUS (↑ cerebral edema risk)
Continue until: pH >7.3, HCO₃ >15, AG closed
▼
Ongoing Fluid Management
Calculate 5–10% deficit → Replace over 24–48 hrs with 0.45–0.9% NS
When BG <300: Add dextrose (D5 or D10 with 0.45% NS) — keep BG 150–300 during infusion
K⁺ Replacement: Add KCl 20–40 mEq/L to fluids once K⁺ <5.5 and UOP confirmed
If K⁺ <3.5: Replace K⁺ BEFORE starting insulin
▼
⚠ CEREBRAL EDEMA WATCH ⚠
Signs: Headache, AMS (especially after initial improvement), vomiting recurrence, Cushing triad, focal neuro signs
IMMEDIATE Treatment: 3% HTS 2–5 mL/kg over 10–20 min OR Mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg
HOB 30° · Reduce IV rate to maintenance · Prepare for intubation (avoid hyperventilation)
MONITOR: BG q1h · BMP + VBG q2–4h · Neuro checks q1h · Strict I&O · ECG for K⁺ changes

Adrenal Crisis
Acute adrenal insufficiency emergency management
REFRACTORY HYPOTENSION + HYPOGLYCEMIA ± HYPONATREMIA ± HYPERKALEMIA
▼
Suspect Adrenal Crisis When:
Known CAH, adrenal insufficiency, or chronic steroid use with intercurrent illness/trauma
Neonate with ambiguous genitalia + electrolyte abnormalities + hypoglycemia
Shock refractory to fluids and vasopressors without clear etiology
Unexplained hyperkalemia + hyponatremia
▼
IMMEDIATE TREATMENT — Do Not Delay for Labs
Hydrocortisone Stress Dose:
Infant: 25 mg IV · Child: 50 mg IV · Adolescent: 100 mg IV
(Or 50–100 mg/m² IV bolus)
Then: 50–100 mg/m²/day divided q6–8h
▼
Fluid Resuscitation
NS or D5NS 20 mL/kg boluses — repeat as needed for hypotension
Use dextrose-containing fluids (D5NS or D10NS) to address concurrent hypoglycemia
Monitor glucose q30 min initially · May need D10W boluses for refractory hypoglycemia
▼
Address Hyperkalemia if Present
Ca gluconate 10% — 0.6 mL/kg IV slow push (cardiac membrane stabilization)
Insulin + Dextrose · Kayexalate (controversial) · Albuterol neb
Hydrocortisone itself helps correct electrolyte abnormalities
REASSESS q15–30 min: BP, Glucose, Electrolytes (Na⁺, K⁺), Perfusion · Response to steroids often dramatic

Toxicologic Emergency (General Approach)
Toxidrome identification and targeted management
SUSPECTED POISONING / TOXIC EXPOSURE
▼
ABCs FIRST — Stabilize Before Decontamination
Protect airway if GCS ≤8 · IV/IO access · Continuous monitoring
Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222
▼
Identify Toxidrome
Sympathomimetic: ↑HR, ↑BP, ↑Temp, dilated pupils, agitation, diaphoresis
Anticholinergic: ↑HR, ↑Temp, dilated pupils, dry skin, urinary retention, delirium
Cholinergic (SLUDGE): Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defecation, GI, Emesis + miosis, bradycardia
Opioid: ↓RR, ↓GCS, miosis · Sedative-Hypnotic: ↓RR, ↓GCS, ↓BP, normal pupils
▼
Decontamination Indicated?
ACTIVATED CHARCOAL
1 g/kg PO (max 50 g)
Within 1–2 hrs of ingestion
Alert, airway intact
Not for: caustics, hydrocarbons, metals, lithium
WHOLE BOWEL IRRIGATION
GoLYTELY via NG
Children: 25 mL/kg/hr
For: sustained-release, body packers, heavy metals
SKIN DECONTAMINATION
Remove all clothing
Wash with soap + water
Provider PPE required
For: organophosphates, chemicals
▼
SPECIFIC ANTIDOTES (See Antidote Table in Chapter 10)
Opioids → Naloxone 0.1 mg/kg · Organophosphates → Atropine + 2-PAM
Acetaminophen → NAC · TCAs → NaHCO₃ · Benzos → Flumazenil (with caution)
Methanol/EG → Fomepizole · Ca-channel blockers → Ca gluconate + high-dose insulin
REASSESS: GCS, Vitals, ECG, Serial Toxicology Levels, Hepatic/Renal Function

Anaphylaxis
Immediate recognition and epinephrine-first approach
ACUTE ALLERGIC REACTION — Multi-System Involvement ± Hemodynamic Compromise
▼
EPINEPHRINE — FIRST AND MOST IMPORTANT TREATMENT
IM Epinephrine 1:1,000 (1 mg/mL): 0.01 mg/kg in anterolateral thigh
Max: 0.3 mg (<30 kg prepubertal) or 0.5 mg (≥30 kg / adolescent)
May repeat every 5–15 minutes if symptoms persist or recur
▼
Simultaneous Actions:
Remove trigger if possible · Position: supine with legs elevated (unless respiratory distress)
High-flow O₂ · IV/IO access · Continuous monitoring · Fluid resuscitation NS 20 mL/kg if hypotensive
▼
ADJUNCTIVE THERAPIES
Albuterol 2.5–5 mg neb for bronchospasm · Diphenhydramine 1 mg/kg IV/IM (max 50 mg)
Ranitidine/Famotidine 1 mg/kg IV (max 50 mg) as H2 blocker
Dexamethasone 0.15–0.6 mg/kg IV (max 10 mg) to reduce biphasic reaction risk
On beta-blockers? → Glucagon 20–30 mcg/kg (max 1 mg) for refractory anaphylaxis
▼
Response to Treatment?
RESPONDING
Observation Period:
1 dose of epi: observe 4–6 hrs
>1 dose of epi: observe 12–24 hrs
Biphasic reaction occurs in up to 20%
REFRACTORY
Repeat IM epinephrine q5 min
Epinephrine infusion 0.1–1 mcg/kg/min
Aggressive fluid resuscitation
Prepare for intubation (early if airway edema)
Transfer to PICU

Pediatric Drowning
Submersion injury assessment and management
SUBMERSION / IMMERSION INJURY
▼
Initial Assessment
ABCs · C-spine precautions if diving mechanism
Core temperature · Duration of submersion · Water type (contamination?)
GCS · Respiratory status · Cardiac rhythm
▼
Breathing / Pulse Present?
NO — CARDIAC ARREST
Full PALS Resuscitation
Prioritize ventilation (primary mechanism = hypoxia)
High-quality CPR · Epinephrine per PALS
If hypothermic: Continue CPR until rewarmed to 32–35°C
"Not dead until warm and dead"
YES — BREATHING
Respiratory Support
Supplemental O₂ → HFNC/NIPPV → Intubation as needed
Continuous SpO₂ and cardiac monitoring
ABG · CXR · BMP · Lactate
▼
Active Rewarming for Hypothermia
Mild (32–35°C): External warming (blankets, Bair Hugger, warm environment)
Moderate (28–32°C): Add warmed IV fluids (40–42°C), warm humidified O₂
Severe (<28°C): Warmed lavage (bladder, gastric), warmed IV fluids, consider ECMO if available
▼
Post-Resuscitation Monitoring
ARDS may develop 6–72 hrs after event — continuous respiratory monitoring
Neurologic assessment · Targeted temperature management per PICU protocol
Prophylactic ABx only if heavily contaminated water · Monitor for secondary infection
REASSESS q30–60 min: Respiratory Status, Temp, GCS, CXR in 6–12 hrs · Transfer to PICU
Prognostic Factors: Submersion <5 min with rapid CPR has better outcomes. Submersion >25 min or core temp <25°C upon arrival generally indicates very poor prognosis but resuscitation should continue until rewarmed.
