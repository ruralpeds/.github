## Structure (10 Major Sections)

## 1. Pathway Overview

**Scope**: All newborns admitted to Level I (well newborn) and Level II (special care) nurseries

### AAP Level of Care Definitions

| Level | Designation | Capabilities |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| Level I | Well Newborn Nursery | Stabilize/care for healthy term newborns 35–37+ wk; stabilize ill newborns until transfer |
| Level II | Special Care Nursery | Care for infants ≥32 wk GA and ≥1500g; provide CPAP, HFNC, IV fluids, gavage feedings, phototherapy; stabilize critically ill infants for transfer |

## 2. Diagnostic Criteria

### Hypoglycemia — AAP Time-Based Thresholds

| Time Period | Screening Trigger | Action Level | Treatment Level |
|-------------|-------------------|--------------|-----------------|
| 0–4 hours | At-risk infant (LPT, SGA, LGA, IDM) | <25 mg/dL → IV dextrose | 25–40 mg/dL → feed + recheck |
| 4–24 hours | Continued screening | <35 mg/dL → IV dextrose | 35–45 mg/dL → feed + recheck |
| >24 hours | As indicated | <45 mg/dL → evaluate | <50 mg/dL persistent → endocrine eval |

### Hyperbilirubinemia — 2022 AAP Guidelines

The 2022 AAP guidelines resulted in approximately 47% reduction in phototherapy thresholds compared to prior guidelines. Key changes:
- Neurotoxicity risk factors formalized
- Escalation and exchange thresholds defined
- Hour-specific bilirubin nomogram integrated
- Universal pre-discharge screening recommended

### Early-Onset Sepsis Risk Stratification

| Approach | Method | Advantages | Limitations |
|----------|--------|------------|-------------|
| Categorical | Risk factors → lab + antibiotics | Simple, well-established | Over-treats, separates dyads |
| EOS Calculator | Multivariate model | Reduces antibiotic use 40–60% | Requires data entry, not validated <34 wk |
| Serial Observation | Vitals q4h × 24h | Avoids labs/antibiotics if well | Requires staffing, may miss subtle sepsis |

## 3. Interventions by Clinical Domain

### Thermoregulation — WHO Classification with Rewarming Protocols

| Classification | Temperature Range | Intervention |
|---------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Normal | 36.5–37.5°C | Routine care |
| Cold stress | 36.0–36.4°C | Skin-to-skin, warm blankets, increase environmental temp |
| Moderate hypothermia | 32.0–35.9°C | Radiant warmer, rewarm 0.5°C/hour, monitor glucose |
| Severe hypothermia | <32.0°C | Aggressive rewarming, transfer to NICU |

### Hypoglycemia Management

1. **First-line**: Oral dextrose gel 40% (0.5 mL/kg buccal) + feed
2. **If gel fails or glucose <25 mg/dL**: D10W 2 mL/kg IV bolus → D10W infusion at 80 mL/kg/day (GIR ~5.5 mg/kg/min)
3. **Persistent hypoglycemia workup**: If unable to maintain glucose >50 mg/dL by 48h or >60 mg/dL thereafter → endocrine evaluation

### Feeding Management

| Setting | Protocol |
|---------|----------|
| Level I | Breastfeeding within 1 hour; formula if indicated; feed every 2–3 hours |
| Level II (late preterm) | Cue-based when stable; gavage if <34 wk PMA or unable to sustain oral feeds; caloric supplementation PRN |

### Respiratory Support

| Modality | Settings | Escalation Trigger |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| Room air | — | SpO₂ <90% or increased WOB |
| Low-flow NC | 0.25–1 LPM | FiO₂ >0.40 or persistent distress |
| HFNC | 2–6 LPM | FiO₂ >0.40 on max flow |
| CPAP | 5–8 cmH₂O | FiO₂ >0.40 on CPAP ≥6 → consider surfactant; failure criteria met → intubation |

## 4. Monitoring Protocols

### Vital Signs with Alert Values

| Parameter | Frequency | Alert Value |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| HR | q3–4h (Level I), continuous (Level II) | <100 or >200 |
| RR | q3–4h (Level I), continuous (Level II) | >60 persistent or apnea >20 sec |
| Temperature | q3–4h | <36.5°C or >37.5°C |
| SpO₂ | As indicated (Level I), continuous (Level II) | <90% |
| BP | As indicated | MAP < GA in weeks |

## 5. Mandatory Newborn Screening

- Metabolic screen (state-specific panel, typically 24–48h of life)
- Universal hearing screen (before discharge)
- CCHD screening (pulse oximetry, ≥24h of life or before discharge)
- Pre-discharge bilirubin (universal screening recommended per 2022 AAP)

---

## 6. Escalation and Transfer Criteria

### Level I → Level II Triggers

- Gestational age <35 weeks
- Birth weight <1800g
- Respiratory support beyond low-flow nasal cannula
- IV fluid/medication requirement
- Phototherapy with risk of exchange transfusion
- Persistent hypoglycemia requiring IV dextrose
- Sepsis evaluation requiring IV antibiotics

### Level II → Level III/IV Triggers

- Gestational age <32 weeks
- Anticipated need for mechanical ventilation >24 hours
- Surgical conditions
- Hemodynamic instability requiring vasopressors
- Therapeutic hypothermia for HIE
- Exchange transfusion

---

## 7. Discharge Criteria

### Physiologic Competencies
- Thermoregulation in open crib ≥24 hours
- Feeding adequacy with sustained weight gain
- Respiratory stability on room air (or stable home O₂ plan)
- Cardiovascular stability

### Pre-Discharge Checklist
- ☐ Metabolic screen completed
- ☐ Hearing screen completed
- ☐ CCHD screen passed
- ☐ Bilirubin within safe range
- ☐ Hepatitis B vaccine administered
- ☐ Car seat tolerance test (if <37 weeks)
- ☐ Safe sleep education documented
- ☐ Follow-up appointment scheduled (within 48 hours of discharge for high-risk)
