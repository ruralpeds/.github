## 1. Newborn Transition Physiology

The transition from intrauterine to extrauterine life represents the most profound physiologic adaptation in human existence, requiring nurses to master a complex interplay of assessment skills, intervention strategies, and evidence-based protocols.

### 1.1 Cardiopulmonary Adaptation

The fetal-to-neonatal cardiopulmonary transition involves the following critical physiologic events:

- **First breath**: Lung fluid clearance, establishment of functional residual capacity (FRC), surfactant activation
- **Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR)**: Rapid decrease with alveolar oxygen exposure and lung expansion
- **Systemic vascular resistance (SVR)**: Increases with clamping of the umbilical cord
- **Ductus arteriosus**: Functional closure typically within 24–72 hours, triggered by increased PaO₂ and decreased circulating prostaglandins
- **Foramen ovale**: Functional closure as left atrial pressure exceeds right atrial pressure

### 1.2 Thermoregulation

Neonates are vulnerable to hypothermia due to high surface-area-to-mass ratio, thin subcutaneous fat, limited brown fat stores (especially in preterm infants), and immature vasomotor control. Four mechanisms of heat loss must be actively managed:

| Mechanism | Description | Prevention Strategy |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Evaporation | Heat loss from wet skin | Immediate drying, skin-to-skin, plastic wrap for preterm |
| Conduction | Heat transfer to cold surfaces | Pre-warmed blankets, warmed surfaces |
| Convection | Heat loss to air currents | Minimize drafts, hat, swaddling |
| Radiation | Heat loss to cold nearby surfaces | Radiant warmer, double-walled incubator |

### 1.3 Metabolic Transition

- Glycogen stores are limited; hepatic gluconeogenesis is immature
- Blood glucose nadir typically occurs at 1–2 hours of life
- Early feeding (within 1 hour) is critical for metabolic stability
- At-risk populations: late preterm, SGA, LGA, IDM

## 2. Initial Assessment and Stabilization

### 2.1 Apgar Scoring

| Component | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|-----------|---|---|---|
| Appearance (color) | Blue/pale | Acrocyanosis | Completely pink |
| Pulse (heart rate) | Absent | <100 bpm | ≥100 bpm |
| Grimace (reflex irritability) | No response | Grimace | Cry/active withdrawal |
| Activity (muscle tone) | Limp | Some flexion | Active motion |
| Respiration | Absent | Weak/irregular | Strong cry |

Scored at 1 and 5 minutes; continue every 5 minutes if score <7 through 20 minutes.

### 2.2 NRP Decision Points

Per NRP 8th edition (2021) / 9th edition (2025):

1. Term gestation? Breathing or crying? Good tone? → Routine care (warm, dry, stimulate, position)
2. If not breathing/gasping or HR <100 → PPV within 60 seconds ("Golden Minute")
3. SpO₂ targets by minute of life:

| Minute of Life | Target SpO₂ |
|----------------|-------------|
| 1 min | 60–65% |
| 2 min | 65–70% |
| 3 min | 70–75% |
| 4 min | 75–80% |
| 5 min | 80–85% |
| 10 min | 85–95% |
