## Section 3: Respiratory Support (Selected Content)

### 3.1 Oxygen Therapy Fundamentals

**Oxygen toxicity risks**:
- Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP): Risk increases with higher oxygen exposures and greater fluctuations
- Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD): Chronic lung disease from oxygen toxicity and barotrauma, defined as oxygen need at 36 weeks PMA
- Increased oxidative stress and cellular damage

**Prevention**: Target saturations 90–95% in preterm; avoid prolonged FiO₂ >50%; minimize fluctuations

### 3.2 CPAP

**Mechanism**: Increases FRC, recruits and stabilizes alveoli, prevents atelectasis, decreases work of breathing, improves oxygenation, splints upper airway

**Settings**:
- Starting pressure: 5–6 cmH₂O (range 4–8, occasionally up to 10)
- Flow: 6–10 L/min (device-specific)
- FiO₂: Titrate to target; wean FiO₂ before pressure

**Nursing care**: Head midline with neck slightly extended; assess nasal septum every 2–4 hours; barrier dressing to protect skin; heated humidification 35–37°C; oral suctioning as needed

**Weaning criteria**: Stable respiratory status, minimal WOB, FiO₂ ≤30–40%, pressure ≤5 cmH₂O

**Troubleshooting**:

| Problem | Causes | Solutions |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Inadequate oxygenation | Prongs displaced, insufficient pressure, worsening disease | Check fit/position, increase FiO₂ or pressure, notify MD |
| Increased WOB | Insufficient pressure, obstructed prongs | Increase pressure, suction, reassess |
| Gastric distension | Air swallowing | OG tube to vent; consider smaller prongs |
| Nasal trauma | Pressure from prongs, improper fit | Reposition/resize, barrier dressing, switch to mask |
| Agitation | Discomfort, hunger | Swaddle, pacifier, feed if appropriate |

### 3.3 High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC)

- Flow rates: 2–8 L/min for neonates
- Provides variable positive pressure, washes out nasopharyngeal dead space, maintains FRC
- Pressure generated depends on flow rate, cannula size, and mouth position
- Indications: Mild-moderate distress, post-extubation support (alternative to CPAP)
