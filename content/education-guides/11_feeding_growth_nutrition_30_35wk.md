## Chapter 1: Developmental Physiology

The GI tract undergoes remarkable maturation during the third trimester. Suck-swallow-breathe coordination does not reliably mature until approximately 34 weeks GA. Even infants born at 34–35 weeks often demonstrate immature feeding behaviors.

**Metabolic demands**: Limited glycogen/fat stores, reduced gluconeogenesis capacity, higher basal metabolic rates per kg. Prone to hypoglycemia in immediate postnatal period.

**Weight loss**: 5–15% of birth weight in first week. Nadir by postnatal day 4–7. Birth weight should be regained by day 10–14.

## Chapter 4: Enteral Feeding Strategies

### Feeding Advancement Protocol by GA

| GA | Trophic Phase | Advancement Rate | Target Full Feeds | Days to Full Feeds |
|----|---------------|-----------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| 30–31 wk | 10–20 mL/kg/day × 1–2 days | 20–30 mL/kg/day | 150–180 mL/kg/day | 7–10 |
| 32–33 wk | 10–20 mL/kg/day × 1 day | 25–35 mL/kg/day | 150–180 mL/kg/day | 5–7 |
| 34–35 wk | May skip; start 30–40 mL/kg/day | 30–40 mL/kg/day | 150–180 mL/kg/day | 3–5 |

Evidence: SIFT trial demonstrated rapid advancement (30 mL/kg/day) was not associated with increased NEC or LOS risk.

### Feeding Routes

| Method | When to Use | Key Considerations |
|--------|------------|-------------------|
| OG gavage | Default for <34 wk; respiratory support | No nasal obstruction; replace per protocol |
| NG gavage | Stable infants; longer indwelling | May increase WOB; leave 3–7 days |
| Bolus gravity (q2–3h) | Default once tolerating advances | Over 15–30 min; mimics physiology |
| Continuous infusion | Severe intolerance, GER, resp distress | Limits hormone cycling; reserve for refractory |
| Oral feeding | Cue-based readiness ≥32–34 wk PMA | Assess readiness before each attempt |

### Cue-Based Feeding Readiness

| Ready (Offer Oral) | Not Ready (Gavage) |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| Quiet alert or drowsy-alert state | Deep sleep or active crying |
| Rooting, hand-to-mouth, sucking on fingers | No oral-seeking behaviors |
| Stable HR, RR, SpO₂ | Tachypnea, desats, increased O₂ need |
| Good tone; flexed posture | Limp, hypotonic, or excessively irritable |

## Chapter 5: Parenteral Nutrition

**PN Quick-Start Reference**:

| Component | Start | Advance | Target/Limit |
|-----------|-------|---------|--------------|
| Amino acids | 2–3 g/kg/day (day 1) | 0.5–1 g/kg/day | 3.5–4.5 g/kg/day |
| Lipids (SMOFlipid) | 1–2 g/kg/day | 0.5–1 g/kg/day | 3–3.5 g/kg/day |
| Glucose (GIR) | 4–6 mg/kg/min | 1–2 mg/kg/min/day | 10–12 mg/kg/min max |

PN weaning begins when enteral feeds reach >50 mL/kg/day; discontinue at ≥100–120 mL/kg/day.

## Chapter 7: Fortification Strategies

| Strategy | Method | Monitoring |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Standard | HMF added at 60 mL/kg/day enteral | Weight gain, labs q1–2 wk |
| Targeted | Milk analysis + adjust to meet targets | Requires milk analyzer |
| Adjustable (BUN-guided) | Increase protein if BUN <10 mg/dL | BUN weekly |

**Human milk hierarchy**: Fresh mother's own milk → Frozen MOM → Pasteurized donor milk → Preterm formula

## Chapter 8: Growth Monitoring

- **Weight gain target**: 15–20 g/kg/day once full feeds established
- **Length**: 0.9–1.1 cm/week
- **Head circumference**: 0.7–1.0 cm/week (proxy for brain growth)
- **Growth charts**: Fenton 2013 or INTERGROWTH-21st
- **Growth faltering**: <15 g/kg/day × 7 days → evaluate caloric intake

## Chapter 9: NEC Prevention (6 Key Interventions)

1. Human milk (preferably MOM; donor milk if unavailable)
2. Standardized feeding protocols
3. Probiotics (institutional decision; evidence evolving)
4. Antenatal steroids
5. Cautious use of H2 blockers/PPIs (associated with increased NEC risk)
6. Lactoferrin (evidence mixed; ELFIN trial negative, but smaller trials positive)

### Feeding Intolerance Assessment

| Benign Findings | Concerning Signs (Hold Feeds) |
|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| Small gastric residuals (<50% of feed volume) | Bilious emesis |
| Occasional emesis, non-bilious | Abdominal wall erythema or tenderness |
| Soft, non-distended abdomen | Abdominal distension >2 cm from baseline |
| Normal stool pattern | Bloody stools |
| Stable vital signs | Systemic instability (apnea, brady, temp instability) |

## Chapter 11: Special Populations

| Population | Key Nutritional Considerations |
|-----------|-------------------------------|
| SGA | Higher protein needs; hypoglycemia risk; accelerated catch-up growth concerns |
| BPD | 120–150 kcal/kg/day; fluid restriction may be needed; caloric density 24–30 kcal/oz |
| CHD | Energy needs 120–150 kcal/kg/day; may need continuous feeds; fluid restriction |
| IDM | Hypoglycemia screening; macrosomia with paradoxically immature systems |
| NAS/NOWS | Caloric needs 130–150 kcal/kg/day; excessive/disorganized sucking; frequent small feeds |

## Chapter 13: Key Principles Summary

1. Human milk is the gold standard; fortification essential for preterm
2. Early aggressive nutrition (PN within 24h, trophic feeds day 1) prevents nutritional deficits
3. Standardized feeding protocols reduce NEC and shorten PN duration
4. Cue-based feeding over rigid schedule-driven protocols
5. Growth monitoring with validated charts (Fenton/INTERGROWTH-21st)
6. Discharge plan must include post-discharge nutritional strategy and follow-up
